# PRD: MaxTech Storage

Storage's requirements, split out of the root `PRD.md` (which holds the shared vision, Lib, config, process and performance sections). Section numbers are the original ones.

## 7. Storage addon requirements

### 7.2 Digital storage (Storage addon, ADR-0010, ADR-0014)

Design round of 2026-10-03 (owner decisions, decision log; the concept and GUI pages are linked from `milestones/M6.md` (milestone notes)). Storage is not an AE2 or Refined Storage copy; where this section differs from those mods, it does so on purpose.

**Ground rules**
- **Standalone:** Storage depends on MaxTech Lib only (ADR-0014).
- **Instant access:** storing and retrieving never wait. Only port, bus and link speed limit automation; player clicks in a terminal are never rate-limited.
- **Simple and early:** a first network (cable, controller, drive, starter cell, terminal, a small power source) is built from a crafting table in the first hours. Deeper mechanics are optional later tiers.
- **Powered by FE:** every network needs power from the start. Storage runs on FE natively until it switches to Lib's energy system before 1.0 (root PRD §5.11, `milestones/MLE.md` (milestone notes)). Devices pay energy per operation (per item moved, per craft pushed), not a flat idle drain; a brown-out slows the network, never wipes it.
- **Server-safe:** rebuild cost and tick time have hard upper bounds whatever the network's size (see "Performance" below).
- **Own GUI style:** Accent Frame, cyan on graphite: dark outline, cyan accent line, a title tab on top, flat monochrome slots, blue-to-cyan gradient bars; pixel art that fits Minecraft.
- **3D models** in the AE2 spirit for cables, drives and terminals, generated like the conduit models with a review page first.

**Network**
- **Channels with hubs (owner decision 2026-10-14, M7N; replaces bandwidth).** Every device uses one channel along its fixed route to the root; controllers and hubs use none. Energy cells, the fuel generator, the creative energy source, display panels and parts, the network anchor, the security module, research terminals, sensors, condition switches (blocks and parts) and crafting CPUs use no channel either and join whenever cables connect them, like AE2's energy cells (owner decisions 2026-10-17). Cable tiers carry 8 / 16 / 32 / 64 channels, each controller root link 64, and a network without a controller 8. A **hub** block (tiers 16 / 32 / 64, no screen, links on every side) gives the devices routed through it a channel budget of their own and uses one channel upstream. Over a limit, only the devices that joined last go offline, and each names the full cable, hub or link. Channels are counted when topology changes, per zone or hub branch, never per transfer or tick. Transfer speed comes only from port, bus and bridge tiers. A server config `network.channels.mode` (`limited` by default, or `infinite`, owner decision 2026-10-18) turns channel limits off: every device gets a channel, loads still show but never full, and zone size and team or subnet device caps still apply.
- **Zones with derived routes.** Players build cables freely. The network is split into zones with a size cap (server config); on a zone rebuild, every device gets one fixed route along the widest cables to the network's root: one of the controller's root links (the lowest device without a controller). Channels are counted along that route, and nothing pathfinds at runtime. Adding a cable attaches it without a rebuild; removing one rebuilds only its zone, lazily and rate-limited. Loops are allowed but give no extra channels.
- **Controller (owner decisions 2026-10-15, M7R; replaces the expansion cluster).** A multiblock of identical controller blocks placed face to face in any shape. Every face of an active block that touches a storage cable or device is a root link (64 channels each); each block adds 10,000 FE buffer and 1,000 FE/t input. The size cap starts at 8 blocks and research raises it to 16, 32 and 64; every step and an absolute maximum are config, and blocks over the cap stay dark. One block holds the network's state; breaking it hands the state to another block, and two controllers that touch merge (the one placed first keeps its state). There is no device limit: channels limit networks. The multiblock is found by a capped flood fill only when a controller block is placed or broken; nothing ticks. Connected textures merge them into one machine, with the frame only on the outside; the same animated face on all six sides (the runic sigil, chosen 2026-10-15). Batch amplifier, efficiency core and surge regulator are upgrade cards in the controller GUI (the device bus is removed with the device limit). Telemetry recorder, ledger module and alarm module are built in (config-gated), shown in the controller GUI and terminals, and research unlocks the views. Security module, antenna mount, wireless access mount, dimension bridge and network anchor are separate network devices placed anywhere on the network; the mounts and the bridge each use a channel, the security module and anchor none (2026-10-17). The capacitor bank folds into the energy cell blocks. The status crown and light strips were removed (owner decision 2026-10-18). A second controller in one network goes on standby (2026-10-14); the subnet controller stays. Plan and open numbers: `milestones/M7R.md` (milestone notes).
- **Older worlds (M7R, done 2026-10-16).** Old cluster saves load as a multiblock controller with their FE, settings, name, alarms and recovery rows. The retired expansion blocks stay registered but inert: batch amplifier, efficiency core and surge regulator blocks turn into their cards, ledger and alarm modules hand their rows and settings to the controller once, a capacitor bank is a tier-1 energy cell, and the devices keep working in place. Research progress on the old cluster nodes carries over to the size nodes, and the regrouped config keys read their old names for one version.
- **Subnet controller.** One block that starts a subnetwork and marks a zone boundary. Per direction it sets a mode (merged, shares up, client, isolated), an optional filter and a priority. The parent mounts the subnet's storage directly through its index, without slot scans.
- **Cables:** four channel tiers, all 4 px wide: a clear glass sleeve with one glowing lane inside it in the cable's colour, the tier shown by the lane's brightness, dim at tier 1 and brightest with a glow at tier 4 (look "J", owner decision 2026-10-15), and on every look as one to four pips on each core face (on the lane's core inside glass sleeves, on the sleeve of covered cables; owner decision 2026-10-15) so the tier reads by shape, not only by brightness (PRD 8.2); cables are not animated, each in plain, covered (opaque sleeve, lane hidden), smart (the lane shows load) and fiber (a brighter dot every 8 px) looks; the earlier glass look is retired and draws like plain, 16 colours plus uncoloured, facades on all. Concept variants kept for later steps: glass data cables (see-through, with light streaks, item glimpses or data bits moving inside, client-animated), framed, wall-bracket, sealed (waterloggable), fiber, lit, armoured, one-way, relay hop, labelled, switch, filter, priority lane, meter, cable spool, patch panel, buried, and a client keybind to hide cables.
- **Antenna links** join distant sites; their range also sets wireless terminal coverage (replaces the access point).
- **Diagnostics:** a network analyzer (hover info; a through-wall overlay coloured by load with routes, channel use, spare links and zone boundaries; click a device to light its route), a controller "Network" tab (power, cluster stats and root links, channels per root link and hub, full cables and hubs, offline devices, warnings with a locate button, a 10-minute load graph), smart cables, and Jade/WTHIT through Lib's `BlockInfoProvider`.

**Capacity: the cell builder**
- A cell is a **casing** (small, medium, large; special: portable, bulk) filled with parts in a **cell workbench**: a **controller chip** (item, fluid, universal at 50 %, creative; chips are the `CellType` registry, so other mods can add them), **memory modules** I–VI (V–VI are endgame) whose capacities add up, and **add-ons** (partition filter with fuzzy mode, void overflow, read-only lock). Priority belongs to the drive, one for all its cells, set on a priority tab shared by every block with a priority (owner decision 2026-10-07; drives, bulk drives and subnet controllers use the whole int range, pattern priority stays -999 to 999, 2026-10-15). The workbench needs no power and no time; parts can be swapped while a cell holds items as long as the contents still fit, and nothing is ever deleted.
- The **starter cell** is a plain crafting recipe, so day one needs no workbench.
- **Capacity is counted in bytes:** each kind of item costs a fixed overhead, and items with data components cost more per kind than plain items.
- Higher memory tiers need the materials of the tier ladder below (tantalite, deep data crystal, void crystal).
- No compression in any form.
- Example builds: starter cell, ore bulk cell, loot cell, trash voider, field cell (portable).

**Materials, machines and progression** (owner decisions 2026-10-03; may still change after playtesting)
- **Goals:** harder than AE2 with its power in mind, but few machines; everything automatable; a semi-realistic electronics theme; competitive with AE2/RS plus their popular addons through a deep endgame.
- **Own resources (five, one per place):** **lumen quartz** ore (Overworld stone and deepslate, common), **tantalite** ore (deepslate, uncommon; refining also yields niobium), **ember quartz** ore (Nether basalt deltas), **deep data geodes** (rare, below y 0; budding data crystal) and **void crystal** geodes (End islands; budding). Ores use normal NeoForge ore features with config, not MaxTech's mixed veins. Vanilla inputs: copper, iron, gold, redstone, glowstone, coal, sand, glass, honey or slime (resin), dried kelp or wool (insulation), and farmed sculk. No loot-only item is required on any progression path (echo shards are optional upgrades).
- **Three production lines meeting at assembly:**
  - *Silicon:* lumen quartz + coal → silicon (furnace) → wafers (lithograph, slice) → doped wafers (lithograph, with redstone; glowstone for advanced) → dies (lithograph, print with a family mask). Slicing kerf re-smelts into silicon.
  - *Metal:* copper → wire (fabricator, draw) → insulated wire (+ dried kelp or wool); iron + copper + lumen dust → signal alloy (furnace) → casing plates (fabricator, press); glass → glass fibre + resin → circuit boards (fabricator, press); tantalite → tantalum + niobium (fabricator, refine) → capacitors; niobium → superconductor wire.
  - *Crystal:* lumen quartz → oscillators (lithograph, slice); quartz dust + glass → display glass (furnace); deep data crystal → data lattice; ember quartz → ember cores (power and speed parts); void crystal → void lattice; sculk → resonator (wireless).
  - Every recipe sits in one of five layers: raw → refined → component → processor → part.
- **Machines (four), each a line's home:** **fuel generator** (FE from any furnace fuel); **lithograph** (slice, dope, print; masks unlocked by research); **fabricator** (draw, press, refine); **assembler** (multi-input assembly of processors, memory modules and devices; a disassembly mode recovers parts). Machines grow through upgrade modules (precision: tantalum, ember: speed, crystal: yield and grades, void: maximum) instead of new machine tiers. All take items and FE on every side; recipes are data-driven JSON with KubeJS schemas. No fluids, no multiblocks among the production machines.
- **Processors:** four families (logic, memory, signal, I/O) in grades basic, advanced (tantalum capacitor) and elite (data lattice); wafer yield per die rises with lithograph modules.
- **Tier ladder** (each tier fixes the previous tier's annoyance; research tasks gate it, materials pay for it):
  - *0 Local:* chest terminal on one chest or barrel (search, sort); lumen quartz, copper; first ten minutes.
  - *1 Network:* controller, drives, starter cells, terminal, ports, four machines; first stored item within about 20–30 minutes of finding lumen quartz.
  - *2 Base:* cable tiers 2–3, zones, crafting terminal, condition switches, displays; tantalite.
  - *3 Reach:* wireless terminal, antennas, P2P, restock belt; sculk resonator, ember quartz for speed and power.
  - *4 Big storage:* memory IV, elite processors, tier-4 superconducting backbones, holo displays; deep data crystal, niobium.
  - *5 Endgame:* memory V–VI (comparable to the largest addon cells), bulk drives, cross-dimension links, top machine modules; void crystal. Autocrafting's large CPUs (M7) use the same tier.
- **Visible growth:** controller glow, drive lights, the lane brightness of cables and tier marks on the GUI frame per tier. A progression tab in the research screen always shows the next tier's tasks, unlocks and material.
- **Difficulty preset** (relaxed, standard, expert) scales recipe amounts, wafer yields and research together.
- **Automation rules:** no stonecutter or other non-automatable vanilla step on a required path; plain crafting recipes work in the vanilla Crafter; every Storage machine and port is hopper- and pipe-friendly; geode and sculk farms are harvested with placer/breaker ports and the collector. The first automation goal the guide teaches is the network's own chip line.

**Research**
- Higher memory tiers, special casings and most add-ons unlock through research, per team.
- **Research tasks** are the backbone: each node offers a few options, and any one of a task, a puzzle or a points cost completes it (owner decision 2026-10-03); a skip token always completes a node whose requirements are done. Tasks use Storage's own mechanics, never specific items; a task referencing an item or tag the pack lacks is skipped. Kind counts scale with the pack's item count and have a cap.
- Points come from discovery (each new kind entering the network), advancements, a machine observer (new recipes seen), a scanner (blocks, mobs, biomes, structures), found notes (loot and librarian trades) and an analyzer (samples). **Skip tokens** come from points (one every N points earned, N scaled by the preset) and, rarely, from structure chest loot.
- Presets `off`, `relaxed`, `standard` (default), `expert`; the tree is datapack JSON, editable through KubeJS.

**Devices**
- **Ports** on any inventory, tank or machine face, in tiers that set items per second; speed cards raise the rate. Plain import, export and storage buses are port presets.
- Interface, P2P tunnels (item, fluid, FE, redstone), facades and owner/team security ship in basic form in M6; more complex versions are decided later. The pattern provider comes with autocrafting in M7 (owner decision 2026-10-03).
- **Energy storage** blocks in three tiers hold FE for the network, filling on surplus and draining on demand; a creative energy source powers a network without limit for testing.
- **Condition switch** (replaces the level emitter): one condition (count above or below, power low, link overloaded, research done) to a redstone output.
- **Display panels** (replace the storage monitor) that merge into wall screens, with **holographic graph displays**: 2D line and bar graphs of any network value in M6, optional 3D plots later.
- **Network chest** (a filtered view of the network as a plain chest), **collector** (pulls item drops in a radius into storage), **placer and breaker** ports.
- **Terminal comfort:** pinned and recent rows, saved tabs, a JEI button that pulls a recipe's ingredients into the inventory, keybinds to deposit everything already stored and to restock the hotbar.
- **Wireless and portable:** wireless terminal (FE-charged, works inside antenna range), auto-restock belt, overflow of pickups into the network when the inventory is full.
- **Multiplayer:** request board, mail port (send items to another player's network), ledger of who took or stored what.
- **Logic layer (M7L prototype, owner decisions 2026-10-07):** a **Logic Controller** on the network holds node graphs (8 graphs of up to 64 nodes, config) built in a node editor. Graphs wake only on events (item or tag counts crossing a limit with a switch-back value, power, finished crafting jobs, redstone into the controller or a labelled device, sensor values, link load, labelled devices added or removed, full storage) and one-shot delays, never on a timer. They read counts, sensor values and device states, check with compare/and/or/not/if, remember with latch, counter and hold, and act on devices addressed by **label** (free text plus colour, per network; subnets keep their own): turn ports, interfaces, providers and sensors off or on, set priority, drive condition switches in "driven by logic" mode, request background crafts, move items between network storage and labelled ports or interfaces through their own handlers, and post notices (network log, team toast, team chat, displays). A **sensor** (block and cable part) reads a foreign block read-only: contents, block state, comparator and redstone, woken by neighbour updates or optional polling. Every node run and sensor read costs checks against a per-team budget (500 per second, config); over it, logic waits and player work goes first. Editing needs the new **logic** access level; every block-facing device checks land claims. Not in the prototype: a sentence view and templates, groups and custom nodes, math nodes, raw block data and pack readers, research gating.
- Kept for later decisions: warehouse and storage matrix multiblocks, bulk drives, crates and docks, accounts and quotas, parallel lanes, network AI, satellite uplink, indexed storage.

- **Autocrafting (extended model, v1):** pattern items, a job planner, and crafting CPUs. `storage.autocraftingMode = extended | classic` (default `extended`). All five extensions below are v1 scope; classic mode disables them and behaves like AE2 (opaque status, cancel-on-failure, untiered CPUs, no stock keeping, black-box dispatch). Both modes share one planner/executor; extended features are config-gated around it.
  1. **Visual job planner.** Requesting a craft shows the full tree: per node, quantity needed / in storage / to craft, assigned provider or machine, estimated duration, red for shortfalls with exact missing counts. Delta-synced to the open terminal as nodes complete. Also viewable for running jobs.
  2. **Tiered CPU cluster.** A free-form cluster like the controller's: a CPU core plus storage, lane and cache blocks touching it, in three tiers on the storage ladder (tantalite, deep data crystal, void crystal; owner decision 2026-10-04, replacing the box multiblock). Tier sets caps: max job size, max parallel lanes, FE/t per active lane. Storage blocks give intermediate buffer, lane blocks parallel lanes, cache blocks retain hot intermediates. CPU draws FE and stalls, not fails, when unpowered.
  3. **Stock keeping.** Stock keeper block and a terminal tab: rules of (key, keep amount, restart threshold, batch size, priority, CPU restriction, redstone enable). Event-driven, submits low-priority background jobs that never pre-empt player requests.
  4. **Machine-aware dispatch.** Processing patterns carry machine category (and recipe when machine-exported). Dispatch picks among interface-attached machines of that category by strategy (`fastest` default, `round_robin`, `lowest_load`), can split batches across machines, and uses recipe duration × overclock for time estimates.
  5. **Partial results and resumable jobs.** On a missing leaf, finish every satisfiable sub-tree, deliver outputs, return all intermediates to storage, mark the job partial with a precise report, and allow resume (auto when the missing key appears, or manual).
  - **Execution details (owner decisions 2026-10-04):** crafting-table recipes run in an assembler block next to a pattern provider. Each pattern input carries a target face (auto-filled for known blocks, overridable), so one provider can feed a furnace's top and side; fluids are normal entries inserted into the face's tank. Providers pull expected outputs from the machine's output face, and anything pushed into an interface counts too. Planning counts the main output only; a chance main output repeats its step until enough has come out, with a cap. Full list: `milestones/M7.md` (milestone notes).
  - Reserved for v2 (design must not preclude): **distributed CPUs** (sub-trees across several CPUs).
- **Pattern creation, two paths:**
  1. **Pattern terminal** (AE2-style): encode crafting or processing patterns by hand or via JEI/EMI recipe transfer (`+` button fills the grid).
  2. **Machine export:** Storage's own machines can write a processing pattern for their selected or last recipe onto a blank pattern in their GUI. The pattern records inputs and outputs by tag and count and the recipe type, so it works in any machine of that type through a pattern provider.
  Both produce the same pattern item format; the CPU does not care where a pattern came from.
- **Storage data model (design from M6, not retrofitted):**
  - Every distinct item/fluid key (type + components) is interned to an `int` ID in a per-network key registry; all hot structures are primitive-keyed (`Int2LongOpenHashMap` totals, `int[]`/`long[]` per cell). Keys with zero total everywhere are released.
  - A network-wide secondary index `keyId → (cell, count)` makes insert-to-existing and extract O(1) instead of walking cells; kept in sync on the same change event that updates totals.
  - Cells persist as a **palette** of unique keys plus a compact `(paletteIndex, count)` array, with a dirty flag so unchanged cells are not reserialised on save.
  - Terminals receive one full dump on open, then `(keyId, count)` deltas; the client builds its own search/sort index so queries never hit the server.
  - Reads (terminals, JEI, bridges) use copy-on-write snapshots of the totals map, so off-thread search/sort is safe and never blocks the tick.
  - `Item → set<keyId>` index for component variants (damaged/enchanted/named) so fuzzy extraction and partition filters are lookups, not scans.
  - The storage index is built on a `StorageProvider` interface from the start (drives, external inventories, AE2/RS bridges are all providers).
- The key types, interning, palette codec, scheduler, snapshots and route tree live in MaxTech Lib (front-loaded before M6 content, `milestones/M6.md` (milestone notes)) so the storage network and autocrafting share them.
- **Performance (on top of §8.1):**
  - **Budget scheduler:** no storage device ticks on its own. One work queue per network runs in the level tick with a time budget from server config; idle devices sleep with growing backoff until an event wakes them; priorities let player requests go before background work. A network over its budget slows down, never the server.
  - **Worker threads:** craft planning, rule checks over large stock, terminal search and sort run on snapshots off the main thread; the main thread only commits results and revalidates them.
  - **Server caps and visibility:** per-team limits on devices, zones, rules and instructions per second; `/maxtech network` lists the heaviest networks with owner, location and tick share; a network over its share is throttled and says why in its controller.
  - **No slot scans of other mods' inventories:** external inventories are read into a cache that is refreshed only on neighbour changes, with backoff for inventories that rarely change.
  - **The cache is a view, never the source of truth:** Storage's own cells are authoritative. Every transfer runs inside a NeoForge transfer transaction against the real handlers (extract, insert, shrink or roll back on a mismatch, commit) and updates counts only by the amounts actually moved. A stale cache can make a display briefly wrong but can never duplicate or lose items. A GameTest covers an inventory emptied behind the network's back.
  - **Batch transfers:** a port moves up to its rate per cadence in one operation, charging energy once; a player's shift-click is one operation.
  - **Listeners subscribe by key id:** condition switches, displays, meters, research tasks and graphs register for the keys they watch; each change is dispatched once.
  - **Collector** registers new item drops through the entity-join event against a spatial index of collector ranges instead of scanning for entities.
  - **Antenna coverage** is precomputed per chunk when a mast is built.
  - **Research progress** is tracked incrementally from the change stream, never by recounting.
  - **Persistence:** cell contents live in level save data keyed by cell id; drives reference cells by id, so an unloaded drive chunk does not remove its cells from the network. Saves rewrite only changed cells (palette format) and serialize from a snapshot.
  - **Client:** terminal updates are merged per key per tick and sent at most about 4 times a second to open terminals only; search runs on a client-side index; animations (displays, holo graphs) stop beyond about 32 blocks, update at most once a second and can be turned off.
  - **Measured:** each network records its own timing (controller tab and admin command). Perf GameTests cover a single cable change on a 10,000-node network, 500 idle ports, a burst insert of 1 million items and saving 100 changed cells, each with a budget.
- **AE2 / Refined Storage bridges** stay a 1.0 requirement (M7b, see §8.2).

### 7.3 Integration with other mods
- Integration with other mods beyond the AE2 and Refined Storage bridges and the KubeJS recipe plugin (M7b, owner decision 2026-10-10) is decided later (owner decision 2026-10-06). Any compat is declared `type = "optional"` in `neoforge.mods.toml`, lives under `compat.<modid>` and loads only behind `ModList.isLoaded`.
